The Scandinavian Defense starts with **1. e4 d5**. Black challenges White’s central pawn on the first move.

## A direct question in the center

After 2. exd5, Black can recapture with the queen. In the line 2...Qxd5 3. Nc3, White develops a knight while attacking that queen. The queen must respond, giving a concrete illustration of gaining time through development.

This does not mean that an early queen move automatically loses. Black can choose a retreat and continue developing. There are also Scandinavian lines beginning with 2...Nf6 instead of an immediate queen recapture.

## Count useful moves rather than queen moves

Chasing a queen is worthwhile when the chase improves your position. A move that attacks the queen but misplaces one of your own pieces may be less useful than calm development.

For Black, the question is whether the queen’s placement and central structure justify the time spent. For White, it is how to turn extra development into coordinated pieces rather than a series of disconnected threats.

## Try it on the board

Play e4, d5, exd5, Qxd5, and Nc3. Notice that the knight’s move does two jobs: it develops and attacks. That combination explains why opening principles emphasize purposeful development.
