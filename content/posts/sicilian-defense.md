The Sicilian Defense begins with **1. e4 c5**. Black controls d4 with the c-pawn instead of mirroring White with ...e5.

## Different structures create different plans

The asymmetry matters. In many Open Sicilian positions, White advances d4 and Black exchanges the c-pawn for it. Black has traded a flank pawn for a central pawn, while White develops quickly and gains room for the pieces.

Those advantages operate on different timescales. Activity can matter immediately; a useful pawn structure may matter more once development is complete.

## A family rather than a single plan

White does not have to enter an Open Sicilian. The moves Nc3 and c3 can lead to different structures and priorities. Recognizing the Sicilian after ...c5 identifies the family, not a complete blueprint for the rest of the game.

Avoid deciding to attack on one wing simply because that is a familiar Sicilian story. The center, king positions, and available pawn breaks should determine the plan.

## Try it on the board

Start with e4 and c5. Observe that Black’s pawn controls d4 without occupying e5. That small difference is the opening’s organizing idea: the two sides can pursue different advantages in the same position.
