The Italian Game begins with **1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 Nc6 3. Bc4**. White develops the king’s bishop to an active diagonal and prepares to castle once the position permits it.

## Why the bishop points at f7

From c4, the bishop looks toward f7. In the starting arrangement, that pawn is defended by Black’s king alone. This makes it a useful tactical landmark, but not an automatic invitation to sacrifice a piece.

A successful attack still needs supporting pieces and a concrete calculation. Black can develop, challenge the center, and address threats rather than waiting for White’s plan to arrive.

## Quiet moves can carry ambition

The Italian does not require an immediate assault. White can support the center with d3 or prepare a later d4 break with c3. The important question is when opening the center will help the pieces already developed.

Moving the same attacker repeatedly while the rest of the army stays home can waste the advantage of an active bishop. Development works best when the pieces begin to cooperate.

## Try it on the board

Reach Bc4 and trace the bishop’s diagonal. Then compare Black’s developing replies ...Bc5 and ...Nf6. Ask how each move changes the next task for White. The opening becomes easier to understand when each move answers a positional question.
